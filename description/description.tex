\begin{abstract}
PRINTEMPS is a metaheuristic solver originally developed for general integer linear programming (ILP) problems. It was extended to handle pseudo-Boolean (PB) problems for the Pseudo-Boolean Competition 2025 \cite{printemps-pb25}. For the Pseudo-Boolean Competition 2026, we submit three solvers: PRINTEMPS itself and two hybrid drivers, \texttt{exact-printemps} and \texttt{scip-printemps}, that run Exact \cite{exact} or SCIP \cite{scip} first and hand the incumbent and fixed variables over to PRINTEMPS as a warm start. We also widened the OPB parser's coefficient range from 32-bit to 64-bit signed integers, although the internal arithmetic remains double-precision and therefore restricts the exactly representable range to roughly 53 bits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
PRINTEMPS (PoRtable INTEger Mathematical Programming Solver) is a metaheuristic solver developed by the first author for general integer linear programming (ILP) problems \cite{printemps}. The PB-specific extension and the rationale of the encoding of nonlinear terms and soft constraints are unchanged from our Pseudo-Boolean Competition 2025 submission, and we refer the reader to that description \cite{printemps-pb25} for the details. This document focuses on what is new for 2026:
\begin{itemize}
\item widened OPB-parser coefficient range from 32-bit to 64-bit signed integers, with a remaining 53-bit limitation on the internal double-precision arithmetic (Sec.~\ref{sec:coeff});
\item the two hybrid solvers \texttt{exact-printemps} and \texttt{scip-printemps} that pair PRINTEMPS with an off-the-shelf complete PB / MIP solver (Sec.~\ref{sec:hybrid});
\item minor PRINTEMPS-side improvements collected since the 2025 submission (Sec.~\ref{sec:other}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Supported Problems}
The submitted solvers target the same six categories as our 2025 submission: \textbf{DEC-LIN}, \textbf{DEC-NLC}, \textbf{OPT-LIN}, \textbf{OPT-NLC}, \textbf{PARTIAL-LIN}, and \textbf{SOFT-LIN}. None of the three submitted solvers participates in the \texttt{-CERT} tracks: PRINTEMPS is heuristic and cannot prove optimality or infeasibility, and although Exact and SCIP are themselves complete, the hybrid drivers do not collect or emit a VeriPB-style certificate.

The hybrid drivers \texttt{exact-printemps} and \texttt{scip-printemps} forward the OPB instance to the first-phase solver as-is. They therefore inherit the input-format support of that solver: in particular, in the nonlinear categories the linearization of products is performed inside Exact / SCIP / PRINTEMPS rather than by the driver.

\section{PRINTEMPS}

\subsection{Computational overview (recap)}
PRINTEMPS searches for solutions to an ILP using Weighted Tabu Search that minimizes an objective function penalized by constraint violations \cite{Nonobe.1998}, together with several ILP-specific enhancements such as instance size reduction, dependent variable extraction, neighborhood filtering, and incremental penalty evaluation \cite{Koguma.2024}. The PB-specific extensions---linearization of products of binary variables and the introduction of slack variables with weighted penalties for soft constraints---are described in detail in our 2025 submission \cite{printemps-pb25} and are reused unchanged.

\subsection{Wider coefficient range}\label{sec:coeff}
In the 2025 submission each OPB coefficient had to fit within the range of a 31-bit signed integer at the parser level. For 2026 the OPB reader and the metadata accumulators have been migrated to \texttt{int64\_t}, so coefficients up to the range of a 63-bit signed integer are accepted by the parser.

Internally, however, PRINTEMPS represents coefficients as IEEE-754 double-precision floating-point values, whose mantissa carries only 53 bits. To avoid silently producing wrong answers when a coefficient cannot be represented exactly, the \texttt{pb\_competition\_2025\_solver} front-end inspects the bit width of the largest absolute coefficient (the \texttt{intsize} field of the OPB metadata) and, if it exceeds 53, prints \texttt{s UNSUPPORTED} and exits before solving. The hybrid drivers inherit this behavior because their second phase invokes the same PRINTEMPS executable. Compared to the 2025 submission this widens the supported range from 31 to 53 bits, but instances with truly 64-bit coefficients remain out of scope.

\subsection{Warm-starting interfaces}\label{sec:warm-start}
To make PRINTEMPS usable as the second phase of a hybrid pipeline, the bundled executable \texttt{pb\_competition\_2025\_solver} gained two new file-based command-line options:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{-i FILE} --- read an \emph{initial solution} as a list of \texttt{xN VALUE} lines. The values are used to seed the local search.
\item \texttt{-f FILE} --- read a list of \emph{fixed variables} in the same format. Variables listed here are pinned to the given value throughout the search.
\end{itemize}
Both inputs are post-processed by PRINTEMPS so that values supplied for original OPB variables are propagated to the auxiliary variables introduced internally during linearization, including negated-literal aliases and product variables.

\subsection{Other PRINTEMPS-side improvements}\label{sec:other}
The most user-visible non-hybrid fixes since the 2025 submission are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a SEGV when importing OPB files containing negated literals in certain positions is fixed;
\item soft inequality constraints whose coefficients are all negative are now imported correctly;
\item infeasibility detected during preprocessing (e.g.\ from contradictory variable bounds) is now reported as \texttt{s UNSATISFIABLE} together with a human-readable message, instead of an unhandled exception;
\item the \texttt{-j} option no longer triggers an infinite loop on some configurations;
\item default values of the time and iteration limits are now ``unlimited'', so the absence of a flag no longer silently truncates the run.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hybrid Solvers}\label{sec:hybrid}

\subsection{Motivation}
PRINTEMPS is a local-search solver and depends strongly on the quality of the starting point. Complete PB / MIP solvers, on the other hand, often find good incumbents and prove a substantial number of variables to be globally fixed during presolve and root-node processing, but may exhaust their time budget before closing the gap on hard optimization instances. The two hybrid drivers, \texttt{exact-printemps} and \texttt{scip-printemps}, are intended to combine the two: spend an initial budget on a complete solver, hand its incumbent and the variables it has proved fixed to PRINTEMPS, and let PRINTEMPS continue to improve the assignment within the remaining time.

\subsection{Two-phase pipeline}
Both hybrid drivers follow the same two-phase shape:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Phase 1 (complete solver).} \texttt{exact-printemps} spawns Exact as a subprocess; \texttt{scip-printemps} drives SCIP in-process via the \texttt{russcip} crate. The instance is solved up to a configurable budget (default 300\,s; capped by the overall wall-clock limit). The phase returns: a verdict; an optional incumbent assignment; the list of variables whose lower and upper bounds coincide after presolve and root-node processing (i.e.\ those proved \emph{globally fixed}); and primal / dual bounds.
\item \textbf{Phase 2 (PRINTEMPS).} The driver invokes the bundled \texttt{pb\_competition\_2025\_solver} on the same OPB instance with the remaining time budget. The incumbent is passed via the \texttt{-i} option of Sec.~\ref{sec:warm-start}; the list of fixed variables is passed via the \texttt{-f} option (gated by the \texttt{--use-fixed-literals} driver flag, which is enabled in the submitted command lines). If both files describe the same variable, the fixed-variable file wins.
\end{enumerate}
If phase 1 returns a \emph{final} verdict---\texttt{OPTIMUM FOUND}, \texttt{UNSATISFIABLE}, or \texttt{SATISFIABLE} on a decision instance---phase 2 is skipped and phase 1's answer is emitted. Otherwise, phase 1's tentative \texttt{s\,\ldots} and \texttt{v\,\ldots} lines are downgraded to PB comments (\texttt{c exact-final:\ \ldots}, \texttt{c scip-incumbent:\ \ldots}) and PRINTEMPS' answer is emitted instead. If PRINTEMPS exits without a feasible solution while phase~1 did find one, the driver falls back to the phase-1 incumbent and synthesizes a \texttt{s SATISFIABLE} / \texttt{v\,\ldots} block from it. By construction the combined stdout contains at most one final \texttt{s} / \texttt{v} block.

\subsection{Solver-agnostic hand-off}
The information exchanged between phases is collected in a single solver-agnostic payload carrying the incumbent, the fixed-variable list, and the primal / dual bounds. Fields are optional, so each phase populates only what it can produce (e.g.\ SCIP additionally supplies a dual bound which is persisted to disk; PRINTEMPS does not currently consume it). The same structure could in principle carry a PRINTEMPS-produced assignment back to a subsequent SCIP / Exact stage as a warm start; this direction is not exercised in the present submission.

\subsection{Signal handling and time budget}
A dedicated thread in the driver listens for \texttt{SIGINT}, \texttt{SIGTERM}, and \texttt{SIGXCPU} and forwards them to the currently active child via \texttt{kill(pid, sig)}; children are placed in their own process group so that a terminal Ctrl-C is not double-delivered. Both Exact and PRINTEMPS gracefully emit their best-known solution on these signals and the driver waits for them to flush before exiting. One known limitation: \texttt{russcip}'s \texttt{solve()} consumes the model, so the driver currently cannot call \texttt{SCIPinterruptSolve} from a side thread; a signal arriving during phase~1 of \texttt{scip-printemps} is therefore observed only after SCIP terminates by its own time limit, and in that case phase~2 is skipped.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}
The current hybrid drivers are deliberately restricted to a simple sequential ``phase 1 then phase 2'' pipeline. Richer compositions we considered but did not implement in time for this submission include: (i) running PRINTEMPS and the complete solver \emph{in parallel} and letting each pull the other's best incumbent as it improves; (ii) \emph{alternating} the two solvers in multiple rounds, so that PRINTEMPS' improved incumbent is fed back to Exact / SCIP as a warm start and its dual bound in turn tightens the search space seen by PRINTEMPS. The \texttt{SolverHandoff} payload is already directional-agnostic, and the SCIP hand-off already carries a dual bound that is currently only persisted to disk; these are the mechanical prerequisites for such an extension. Wiring the loop itself, together with a matching flag on the PRINTEMPS binary to consume a dual bound, is left for future work.

\subsection{Build and packaging}
The hybrid drivers form a single Rust crate that produces the two binaries \texttt{exact-printemps} and \texttt{scip-printemps}. The SCIP integration is gated behind a Cargo feature so that the Exact-based binary builds on hosts without SCIP installed. The submitted Linux binaries are built with static linking: Exact and \texttt{pb\_competition\_2025\_solver} are fully statically linked; SCIP and SoPlex are linked statically into \texttt{scip-printemps} (with \texttt{-DSHARED=OFF}), while \texttt{glibc}, \texttt{libstdc++}, and \texttt{libgomp} remain dynamically linked.

\section{Conclusion}
For the Pseudo-Boolean Competition 2026 we submit PRINTEMPS together with two hybrid drivers, \texttt{exact-printemps} and \texttt{scip-printemps}, that respectively pair PRINTEMPS with Exact and SCIP. The PB-specific extensions of PRINTEMPS itself are inherited unchanged from the 2025 submission; the main additions are the wider coefficient range, file-based warm-starting interfaces, and the two-phase hybrid drivers built on top of them.
